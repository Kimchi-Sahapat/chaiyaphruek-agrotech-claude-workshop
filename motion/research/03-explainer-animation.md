# 03 · Explainer Animation — what "good" looks like

> Part of the motion research pack ([index](README.md)). Researched Nov 2026.
> **Verification note:** several primary pages (wyzowl.com, PMC, pg.ucsd.edu) were blocked by the session proxy, so many claims were checked against search-engine extracts of the primary source, not the full text. Items marked ⚠️ are secondary, contested, or our own inference.

## TL;DR — the 10 rules we adopt

1. **One message, one practice per video.** Write it as one Thai sentence before scripting.
2. **Spine:** symptom farmers recognise (real photo, first 3 s) → *why* it happens → what to do (≤ 5 steps) → *when* (stage/month) → proof from a real orchard → CTA (LINE @cyp-agro "ส่งรูปมาถามได้").
3. **Mechanism before steps** — farmer-video research shifts emphasis "from how to why".
4. **Real local photos/footage as the base layer**, flat diagram overlays (arrows, labels, cut-aways) on top = our *photo-real hybrid* style. No clip-art farmers.
5. **Voice-over carries the explanation; on-screen text = keywords only** (≤ 35 Thai chars/line, ≤ 2 lines) so it still works with sound off.
6. **Signal one thing at a time** — arrow, ring, zoom, numbered step counter "1/3".
7. **Animate only what changes.** Stage transitions; end on a **static recap card**.
8. **Slow, warm, conversational Thai** (≈3.5–4.2 syllables/s ⚠️), human voice preferred.
9. **Every agronomic claim, rate and timing comes from the label or DOA** — else a placeholder + tell the user.
10. **Pre-test with 3–5 farmers** on a mid-range phone: can they retell the steps?

## 1. Structure & scripting

- **Studio default:** Hook → Problem → Solution → How it works → Proof → CTA (industry habit, not research-tested). Short-form split ≈ 2 s hook, 15–25 s body, 3–5 s CTA ([Stackmatix](https://stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-creative-best-practices-2026)).
- **Common Craft (Lee LeFever): context before detail** — low-detail big picture first, then add detail ([Common Craft](https://news.commoncraft.com/posts/what-jpegs-can-teach-us-about-learning)).
- **Farmer-video tradition:** learner-centred videos shift emphasis **"from how to why"**, teaching the principle behind the practice ([Van Mele et al. 2010](https://agroinsight.com/downloads/Articles-Agricultural-Extension/6_JAEE%20-%20Video%20in%20cross-cultural%20learning%20-%20Van%20Mele%20et%20al2010.pdf)).
- **Our spine** (synthesis — mirrors how the shop sells: ask crop → stage → symptom first):
  `อาการที่เห็น (hook) → ทำไมถึงเป็น → ทำอย่างไร (≤5 ขั้น) → ทำเมื่อไร (ระยะ/เดือน) → หลักฐานจากสวนจริง → CTA`
- **One-message rule** is supported by Mayer's *coherence* principle: removing extraneous material improved learning in 23/23 tests, median d = 0.86 ([Cambridge Handbook chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/CD5B7AE1279A9AB81F8EEBB53DBEC86E)).

### Length by channel

| Channel | Target | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok / FB-IG Reels | 20–45 s for one fact · 60–90 s for a 3–5-step process | TikTok data (~2021 ⚠️): 1 in 4 top videos were 21–34 s ([MMR](https://mobilemarketingreads.com/?p=20237)) |
| LINE OA | 30–90 s, one task | inference ⚠️ — link to the long version |
| YouTube / website | 2–6 min | Wistia 2016 (564,710 videos): steepest drop after 2 min ([Wistia](https://wistia.com/blog/optimal-video-length)); Guo et al.: < 6 min |
| Group screening / training | 10–15 min | Access Agriculture standard ([AESA](https://aesanetwork.org/enhancing-the-potential-of-quality-videos-for-farmers/)) |

### Voice-over pace
- English: 125–160 wpm (≈150 default) ([Stimme24](https://www.stimme24.com/en/guide/explainer-video-script/)).
- **Thai:** no industry VO standard found. Best data: Coupé et al. 2019 (*Science Advances*) — Thai read-aloud ≈ **4.7 syllables/s** (Japanese 8.0, Spanish 7.7); information rate converges ≈ 39 bits/s across languages ([CNRS](https://www.cnrs.fr/en/press/similar-information-rates-across-languages-despite-divergent-speech-rates)).
- ⚠️ Our working target for older farmers: **3.5–4.2 syllables/s** (≈ 210–250 syllables/min) with pauses between steps. *Budget Thai scripts in syllables or seconds, not words* (no spaces). Time a real read-aloud to confirm.

### Wyzowl State of Video Marketing 2026 (vendor survey, mostly Western ⚠️)
91% of businesses use video · 68% of video marketers make explainers · 96% of people have watched an explainer to learn about a product · 63% prefer a short video to learn about a product (articles 12%, infographics 7%) · 85% persuaded to buy by a video ([Wyzowl](https://wyzowl.com/sovm-results-2025/)). Several secondary sites misquote 63% as 73–78%.

## 2. Learning science (Mayer's 12 principles → explainer practice)

| Principle | Definition | In our explainers |
|---|---|---|
| Multimedia | Words + pictures beat words alone | Every VO sentence has a matching visual |
| Coherence | Cut extraneous words/pictures/sounds | No music bed or decorative icons under the explanation |
| Signaling | Cue the organisation of key material (24/28 tests, d = 0.41) | Arrows, rings, "1/3" step counters, gold on ONE word |
| Redundancy | Graphics + narration beat graphics + narration + identical on-screen text | Keyword captions, not a transcript |
| Spatial contiguity | Words next to the thing they describe | Label the root *on* the root |
| Temporal contiguity | Narrate while it is on screen | VO timestamps drive keyframes (cue sheet) |
| Segmenting | Learner-paced chunks beat one stream | Series of short videos, chapter cards, pause beats |
| Pre-training | Teach key names first | Primer: stage names, "ไฟทอปธอร่าคืออะไร" |
| Modality | Spoken narration beats printed text beside graphics | VO carries the explanation |
| Personalization | Conversational beats formal | "พี่ ๆ ชาวสวน", not textbook Thai |
| Voice | Friendly human voice beats machine voice | Staff/farmer voice; pre-test any TTS |
| Image | Showing the speaker doesn't necessarily help learning | Presenter for trust + hook; cut away to visuals |

Mayer groups them by load type ([Dartmouth](https://services.dartmouth.edu/TDClient/1806/Portal/KB/PrintArticle?ID=171655)): coherence → temporal contiguity reduce *extraneous* load; segmenting, pre-training, modality manage *essential* load; multimedia, personalization, voice, image foster *generative* processing.

- **Cognitive load theory:** working memory is small — cut design clutter, chunk hard topics, prompt sense-making. *Expertise reversal* ⚠️: experienced growers get bored by basics → keep basics in a separate primer.
- **Brame (2016)**, *CBE—Life Sciences Education* 15:es6 ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5132380/)): manage cognitive load (signaling, segmenting, weeding, audio/visual split); maximise engagement (brief, targeted, conversational, enthusiastic); promote active learning (questions, follow-ups). Engagement ≈ 100% for videos < 6 min, ≈ 50% at 9–12 min, ≈ 20% at 12–40 min (citing Guo).
- **Guo, Kim & Rubin (2014)** — 6.9 M edX sessions ([PDF](https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf)): shorter is better (< 6 min); talking head + slides beats slides; Khan-style drawing beats PowerPoint; invest in pre-production; personal feel beats studio polish. Fast speakers (≈ 254 wpm) held students longer ⚠️ — **not safe for older farmers**.

**Transfers to short social video:** coherence, signaling, contiguity, segmenting-as-series, conversational tone, brevity, personal feel.
**Doesn't transfer cleanly:** (a) studies used viewers who chose to watch — in a feed the first job is stopping the scroll; (b) fast speech; (c) redundancy collides with **sound-off viewing** — Facebook said most feed video starts muted, 41% of its video ads made no sense without sound and captions raised view time ≈ 12% (2016–17 data ⚠️, [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-adds-automated-captions-for-video-ads-offers-tips-to-improve-vide/452401)). **Compromise: VO + short keyword-level Thai captions burned in.**

## 3. Video for farmers — the evidence

**Digital Green** (participatory video, local farmers on camera)
- Pilot (Gandhi et al. 2009, 1,470 households): ≈ 7× adoption vs training-and-visit extension, ≈ 10× cheaper per adoption — the organisation's own data ⚠️ ([paper](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2009/03/322-797-2-PB.pdf)).
- Bihar RCT (280 villages): yields +12–18%, profits +9–24%, ≈ US$3.50 vs US$35 per adoption; did *not* raise SRI adoption beyond conventional extension ([J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/case-study/video-based-support-small-scale-farmers-around-world?lang=en)).
- Ethiopia RCT (Abate et al., *World Development* 2023): higher uptake, +35% attendance at extension sessions ([IFPRI](https://essp.ifpri.info/2022/01/03/accelerating-technical-change-through-icts-evidence-from-a-video-mediated-extension-experiment-in-ethiopia/)).
- **Why it works:** people like me on screen (homophily), local dialect, a facilitator repeating the non-negotiables ([World Bank](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/information-intervention-visit-digital-green)).

**Access Agriculture / Agro-Insight (Bentley, Van Mele)**
- Quality = scientific + farmer knowledge, clear stable images of real practice, never promotes a product ([AESA](https://aesanetwork.org/enhancing-the-potential-of-quality-videos-for-farmers/)).
- Ghana cocoa participatory videos (incl. **black pod — a *Phytophthora* disease, closest analogue to durian root rot**): adopters +20–40% yield, −10–20% pesticide, +23–55% income ([IITA](https://biblio1.iita.org/handle/20.500.12478/2541)).
- Method "zooming-in, zooming-out": study contexts, film with local farmers, test in several contexts, then scale ([Van Mele](https://www.agroinsight.com/downloads/Articles-Agricultural-Extension/5_RDN%20-%20Zooming-in%20zooming-out%20-%20Van%20Mele%202010.pdf)). How-to series: [accessagriculture.org/how-to-produce-videos](https://Accessagriculture.org/how-to-produce-videos).

**SAWBO — Scientific Animations Without Borders** (animation for low-literacy farmers)
- Benin: localized animations → higher test scores and retention than live extension presentations ([ITD 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/titdxx/v24y2018i2p224-244.html)).
- Burkina Faso RCT (48 villages): phone animations matched live demos for learning and for adoption of *known* technologies; **for new technologies, live demos did better** (p < .10) ([EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:taf:titdxx:v:24:y:2018:i:3:p:429-460)).
- Mozambique: 2 years later 97% remembered the steps, 89% adopted ([MSU](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/animated-videos-advance-adoption-of-agriculture-techniques)).
- Design: speech + images carry content, not on-screen text; recognisable but culturally neutral backgrounds (because SAWBO reuses videos across countries — **we need the opposite: hyper-local**). Early work was judged "informative but not attractive" → attractiveness matters. Accuracy enforced (anatomically correct pests).

**Review of 43 African studies:** video works best in local languages with a facilitator; 65.1% showed high knowledge gains, 51.2% behaviour change ([Purdue](https://purr.purdue.edu/publications/4906)).

**Thai farmers & media:** young farmers (Mae Chaem, n=369): 90% use LINE, 89% Facebook, 86% search farming info on YouTube ([Khamtavee 2024](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ags:reowae:343523)) — but a broader Chiang Mai sample (n=365) found 81.9% did **not** use social media ([Kanjina 2021](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ags:ajosrd:342342)). Chumphon durian farmers most valued Facebook posts that help with productivity and pest decisions ([MCI](https://so07.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/MCI/article/view/1616)).

**What made farmer videos effective:** local language/accent · peer farmers in real fields · one practice, step by step, hands doing the work · the *why* · repeating non-negotiables · pre-testing · non-promotional tone · real demonstration alongside animation for *new* practices.

## 5. Style taxonomy → our choice

| Style | Strength | Weakness | Use for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2D flat / character | cheap to revise | generic/childish; clashes with real-photo CI | abstract ideas only; **no cartoon farmers** |
| Infographic / motion graphics | numbers, schedules | overload risk | calendars, verified timings |
| Whiteboard | step-by-step logic | plain | e.g. C/N ratio logic |
| Isometric / 2.5D | systems/layouts | abstract for older viewers | drainage layout, delivery flow |
| **Cut-out / mixed media with real photos** | distinctive, grounded | needs a photo library | **DEFAULT** |
| 3D | invisible processes | cost; heavy in HTML | fake with layered 2D cross-sections |
| UI / screencast | exact steps | dates quickly | LINE "send a photo" flow |
| Live action | trust | logistics | proof shots, testimonials |

**Decision: "photo-real hybrid"** — base layer real Chanthaburi photos/footage (farmers, trees, packs); overlay flat diagram elements (arrows, labels, root-zone cut-away, stage timeline) + animated Thai keyword captions; brand green for frames/accents; overlays in **white or gold** (green lines vanish on foliage).

## 6. Production workflow

Studio sequence: brief → script → written storyboard → style frames → visual storyboard → **VO recorded before animation** → animatic → animation → sound → review; sign-off at each stage ([Yum Yum](https://www.yumyumvideos.com/blog/explainer-video-production-process/)).

**A small team cannot skip:** (1) one-line brief (message, viewer situation, channel, KPI); (2) script fact-checked vs label/DOA; (3) rough VO + timing lock — *in our HTML pipeline the timeline with placeholder art IS the animatic*; (4) sound-off check on a small phone; (5) pre-test with 3–5 farmers ("retell the steps").
**Can compress:** style frames (reusable template), full storyboards (thumbnails are enough), custom music.

## 7. Visualising processes & data

- Animate only what changes over time; animations fail when too fast/complex ([Tversky et al. 2002](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs448b/papers/Tversky_AnimationFacilitate_IJHCS02.pdf)).
- Change one thing at a time — staged transitions improve chart reading ([Heer & Robertson 2007](https://idl.uw.edu/papers/animated-transitions)); for comparisons, static small multiples beat animation ([Robertson et al. 2008](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~stasko/papers/infovis08-anim.pdf)) → **end on a static summary frame**.
- Honest charts: Tufte's lie factor ≈ 1; bars start at zero; don't scale icons by area; ≤ 4 bars, direct labels ([Graphical integrity](https://www.cs.usfca.edu/~apjoshi/cs686-grad-vis/lectures/Graphical_Integrity.pdf)).
- **Step sequences:** numbered (1/3, 2/3, 3/3), one step per beat, same framing, hands doing the work, recap card.
- **Life cycles:** ring diagram with a "you are here" marker tied to month + rain.
- **Before/after:** same framing, same light, dates labelled.
- **Timelines:** farmers' own stage words (ใบเพสลาด, มะเขือพวง, ปริหางปลา…) — confirm order with DOA.
- **Healthy vs diseased:** never red-vs-green colour alone (colour-blind viewers) — add icons/labels.
- **On-screen Thai text:** ≤ 35 characters per line excluding above/below-line marks, ≤ 2 lines ([Netflix Thai Timed Text Style Guide](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/220448308)).

## Do / Don't

**Do** — one practice per video · open on a recognisable symptom (real photo) in 3 s · symptom → why → ≤5 steps → when → proof → CTA · mechanism before steps · conversational Thai + farmers' stage words · human voice · VO-driven cue sheet · labels on the object · keyword captions ≤35 chars × 2 lines · highlight one thing at a time · real photos as base, schematics only for the invisible · end on a static recap card · honest charts · make a series · pre-test with farmers · source every rate/timing from the label or DOA · state channel + aspect ratio.

**Don't** — several practices or a product list in one explainer · music/SFX/busy backgrounds under narration · full narration as on-screen paragraphs · fast speech · clip-art/stock non-local people · animating static facts for movement's sake · cut-off chart axes, 3D charts, area-scaled icons · red-vs-green only · animation alone for a brand-new practice (pair with real demo footage) · hard-sell (knowledge first; product as one option at the end).
